# Working While Receiving Social Security Disability: Gross Income Limits and Work Incentives (2026)

## Introduction

If you receive Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI), you may be able to work while continuing to receive benefits. However, there are strict income limits and rules you must follow. This guide explains how much you can earn without losing your benefits, including special rules for self-employment.

---

## Substantial Gainful Activity (SGA)

**Substantial Gainful Activity (SGA)** is the level of work activity and earnings that Social Security considers "substantial" and "gainful." If your earnings exceed the SGA threshold, you may be denied benefits or have your benefits terminated.

### 2026 SGA Limits

| Disability Type | Monthly SGA Limit |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Non-blind individuals | **$1,690** |
| Statutorily blind individuals | **$2,830** |

**Example:**
- Maria earns $1,600/month working part-time as a non-blind individual → **Under SGA** → Benefits continue
- Maria earns $1,700/month → **Exceeds SGA** → Benefits may be affected

**Important Notes:**
- SGA is measured in **gross pay** for wage earners
- For self-employed individuals, SGA is based on **net earnings** (after business expenses)
- The SGA amount is adjusted annually based on the national average wage index

---

## Trial Work Period (TWP)

The **Trial Work Period (TWP)** allows you to test your ability to work for up to **9 months** while receiving full SSDI benefits, regardless of how much you earn.

### 2026 TWP Threshold

| Month Type | Earnings Trigger |
|------------|------------------|
| TWP Service Month | **$1,210 or more** in gross earnings |

### TWP Rules

1. **No earnings limit**: You can earn any amount during a TWP month and still receive full benefits
2. **9 months total**: You get 9 TWP months within a rolling **60-month (5-year) period**
3. **Months don't need to be consecutive**: You can use TWP months sporadically
4. **Must report work activity**: You are required to report any work to Social Security

### Self-Employment TWP Rules

For self-employed individuals, a month counts as a TWP service month if **either**:
- Your net earnings exceed **$1,210** in that month, **OR**
- You work **more than 80 hours** in that month (regardless of earnings)

**Example:**
- John starts a small online business and works 90 hours in March but earns only $800 net → **Counts as a TWP month** because he worked 80+ hours
- In April, John works 40 hours and earns $1,500 → **Counts as a TWP month** because earnings exceed $1,210

---

## Extended Period of Eligibility (EPE)

After completing your 9 TWP months, you enter a **36-month Extended Period of Eligibility (EPE)**. During this period, your benefits are evaluated monthly based on SGA.

### EPE Rules

1. **Benefits for under-SGA months**: You receive benefits for any month your earnings fall below SGA ($1,690 non-blind / $2,830 blind)
2. **Benefits suspended for over-SGA months**: If your earnings exceed SGA in a month, you don't receive benefits for that month
3. **Grace period**: The first time you exceed SGA in the EPE, you receive benefits for that month plus the following 2 months
4. **After EPE**: If you continue earning above SGA after the 36-month EPE ends, your benefits will typically terminate

**Example:**
- After TWP, Sarah works part-time:
  - January: Earns $1,500 (under SGA) → **Receives benefits**
  - February: Earns $1,800 (over SGA) → **No benefits for February**
  - March: Earns $1,400 (under SGA) → **Receives benefits again**

**Key Point**: During the EPE, if you lose your job or your earnings drop, contact Social Security immediately to reinstate your benefits (no new application needed if within the 36-month period).

## Impairment-Related Work Expenses (IRWE)

**Impairment-Related Work Expenses (IRWE)** are costs you pay for items or services that you need in order to work because of your disability. These expenses can be deducted from your gross earnings when determining if you've exceeded SGA.

### Qualifying IRWE Expenses

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| Attendant care services | Assistance traveling to/from work, help with personal functions at work |
| Medical devices | Wheelchairs, prosthetics, hearing aids, inhalators, pacemakers |
| Transportation modifications | Vehicle modifications for disability-related commuting |
| Service animals | Guide dogs, service animals (purchase, training, food, veterinary costs) |
| Medical equipment | Durable medical equipment needed for work |
| Medications | Prescription drugs necessary to control your condition (routine medications not deductible) |

### IRWE Requirements

- You must **pay for the item/service yourself** (not reimbursed by Medicare, Medicaid, or private insurance)
- The item/service must be **necessary for you to work** due to your disability
- The cost must be **reasonable** (standard charge in your community)
- You must **keep receipts** and proof of payment

**Example:**
- David earns $1,850/month (over $1,690 SGA limit)
- David pays $250/month for specialized transportation to work due to his disability
- **SGA Calculation**: $1,850 - $250 = **$1,600** (under SGA)
- **Result**: David's benefits continue because IRWE deductions bring his countable earnings below SGA

## Self-Employment Rules

Self-employment is treated differently from wage employment under Social Security rules. If you're self-employed and receiving SSDI, additional evaluation criteria apply.

### How Self-Employment Is Evaluated

Social Security uses either the **Three-Tests Framework** (general evaluation) or the **Countable Income Test** (after 24 months of benefits).

#### Three-Tests Framework

Social Security applies these three tests in order. **Only ONE test needs to trigger for a finding of SGA.**

**Test One: Significant Services and Substantial Income**
- Are you providing **significant services** to the business?
- Is your income **substantial** (above SGA threshold)?
- Working **more than 45 hours per month** on management activities may indicate significant services
- If both conditions are met → **SGA**

**Test Two: Comparability of Work Activity**
- Is your work **comparable** to that of unimpaired individuals in similar businesses?
- Comparability is measured by: hours, skills, energy output, efficiency, duties, and responsibilities
- Even if your income is low, if your work output matches unimpaired peers → **SGA**

**Test Three: Worth of Work Activity**
- Is your work clearly **worth more than the SGA amount** when considering its effect on the business?
- Compare to what an employer would pay an employee for similar duties
- Even if the business is unprofitable, if your labor has market value above SGA → **SGA**

**Example (Test Three):**
- You own a graphic design business and work 20 hours/week
- Due to disability, you only accept a few clients
- However, the work you complete would cost a client $3,000/month to outsource
- Social Security may find your work is worth more than SGA → **SGA**

#### Countable Income Test (After 24 Months of Benefits)

For title II disability beneficiaries who have received benefits for **at least 24 months**, Social Security uses the simpler Countable Income Test:

1. Calculate **Net Earnings from Self-Employment (NESE)**
2. Deduct:
   - Value of **unpaid help** from family/others
   - **IRWE** (if not already deducted as business expense)
   - **Unincurred business expenses** paid by another person/agency
3. What remains is your **Countable Income**
4. Compare Countable Income to SGA threshold

**Example:**
- Lisa has a home-based craft business
- Monthly net earnings: $2,000
- Her spouse provides unpaid help valued at $400/month
- She has IRWE of $200/month
- **Countable Income**: $2,000 - $400 - $200 = **$1,400**
- **Result**: Under $1,690 SGA → Benefits continue

### Self-Employment Documentation Requirements

If you're self-employed, you must report:
- Monthly profit/loss statements
- Annual net profits estimate
- Hours worked each month
- Any unpaid help (names, hours, work completed)
- Business expenses (including those provided at no cost)
- Any IRWE related to your business

### Special Situation: Unprofitable Businesses

Even if your business shows a loss, you may still be performing SGA:
- Working **80+ hours/month** counts as a TWP service month (even with $0 profit)
- Under the Three-Tests Framework, Test Two (Comparability) or Test Three (Worth) may find SGA based on your labor value, not business profitability

**Tip**: Keep detailed records of hours worked, tasks performed, and any assistance received. Documentation is critical for self-employment cases.

## Other Work Incentives

### Plan to Achieve Self-Support (PASS)

A **PASS** is a written plan approved by Social Security that allows you to set aside income or resources to reach a work goal. Money set aside under a PASS is **not counted** when determining your SSI payment amount.

**Example:**
- You want to start a computer repair business
- You set aside $200/month from your SSDI check for training and equipment
- This $200 is excluded from countable income → Your SSI payment increases

### Subsidy

A **subsidy** is extra support from your employer due to your disability, such as:
- Extra paid breaks
- Reduced workload compared to co-workers
- Special accommodations that reduce your actual work output

If you receive a subsidy, Social Security will determine its value and may adjust your SGA calculation upward, allowing you to earn more before exceeding SGA.

### Unsuccessful Work Attempt (UWA)

If you **tried to work** but had to stop within **6 months** due to your disability, those months may not count as SGA. This protects beneficiaries who attempt work but find they cannot sustain it.

**UWA Requirements:**
- Work lasted 6 months or less
- Work stopped or reduced below SGA due to your impairment
- You made bona fide efforts to continue working

## Medicare Coverage After Benefits Stop

If your benefits stop due to work, you can continue **Medicare coverage** for at least **93 months (7 years, 9 months)** after your TWP ends. This includes:
- **Part A** (Hospital Insurance) - No premium required
- **Part B** (Medical Insurance) - You pay a monthly premium
- **Part D** (Prescription Drug Coverage) - You pay a monthly premium

You must **request** continued Medicare coverage; it doesn't happen automatically.

---

## Reporting Requirements

You are **required by law** to report:
- Any changes in your work activity
- Changes in your earnings
- Changes in your ability to work

**How to Report:**
- Online: My Social Security account at ssa.gov
- Phone: 1-800-772-1213
- In person: Your local Social Security office (call first for appointment)

**Failure to report** can result in overpayments that you must repay.

## Key Takeaways

1. **You CAN work while receiving SSDI** - The system is designed to encourage trial work
2. **TWP has no earnings limit** - Earn as much as you want during your 9 TWP months
3. **After TWP, SGA limits apply** - Stay under $1,690/month (non-blind) or $2,830/month (blind)
4. **Self-employment has special rules** - Use the Three-Tests Framework or Countable Income Test
5. **IRWE can help** - Deduct disability-related work expenses from your earnings
6. **Document everything** - Keep records of earnings, hours, expenses, and assistance
7. **Report all work activity** - Failure to report can result in overpayments
