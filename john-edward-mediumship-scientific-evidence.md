# John Edward: Scientific Evidence for Mediumship

While James Randi was busy exposing spoon-benders like Uri Geller, a young man on Long Island was discovering his own gift for connecting with the deceased. John Edward would go on to become perhaps the most scientifically tested medium in history.

---

## Career Overview

John Edward McGee Jr. (born 1969) first discovered his abilities at age 15 when a psychic reading convinced him he could communicate with the dead. He developed his skills reading at fairs and eventually launched the television show that would revolutionize the genre: "Crossing Over with John Edward." [1, 2]

### Crossing Over: A Television Phenomenon

"Crossing Over with John Edward" premiered on the Sci-Fi Channel in 2000 and became a television sensation:

* **First syndicated show** focused entirely on psychic mediumship—pioneering the genre worldwide [1, 2]
* **Ran for four seasons** (2000-2004) and was syndicated to over 100 countries [1, 2]
* **Global reach**: Broadcast in the US, UK (on Living TV), Australia, and numerous other countries [2]
* **Pioneering format**: First TV show devoted to psychic mediumship to achieve worldwide syndication [2]

The show's format evolved and improved with each iteration:

1. **Crossing Over with John Edward** (Sci-Fi Channel, 2000-2004) - The original that pioneered the genre
2. **John Edward Cross Country** (WE tv, 2006-2009) - A follow-up series that traveled across America [23]

Both shows featured Edward giving psychic readings to studio audience members, with messages from deceased loved ones that audience members would validate and confirm. [1]

His career includes:

* Multiple New York Times bestselling books ("One Last Time," "What If God Were the Sun," "Crossing Over")
* **"Best Psychic on Long Island"** - Best of Long Island Press (won 2010, 2012, 2013; runner-up 2011, 2014) [24]
* **People Magazine** - Named one of "Most Intriguing People of the Year" [25]
* Sold-out seminars and lectures worldwide
* A waiting list for private readings that stretched over two years
* Recent project: EvolvePlus.Tv, an online platform for metaphysical education

### A Note on Popularity

Even critics took notice—John Edward was featured in a **South Park** episode titled "The Biggest Douche in the Universe" (2002), where he was crowned just that. [26] Given the show's reputation for brutal satire, being featured at all is perhaps the ultimate tongue-in-cheek compliment to his cultural impact and fame.

### Why He Left American Television

Despite its initial success, "Crossing Over with John Edward" was canceled in 2004 after three seasons:

**Low Ratings**: The show averaged only a 0.9 rating in syndication, down 31% from the previous year. It "languished in sub-1.0 territory" for the first three months of the 2003-04 season. [27][28]

**Format Changes Didn't Help**: The show underwent a makeover at the beginning of the 2003-04 season to attract more viewers, but those changes didn't have the effect Universal was looking for. [27]

**Conflict with Production Company**: In a 2006 interview with TV Guide, Edward explained his move to WE tv: "at Universal it was, 'Shut up. You're the talent. Let us do what we do.' They respect me as a producer and as a person, whereas at Universal it was, 'Shut up. You're the talent. Let us do what we do.'" [29]

**WTC Controversy**: In 2001, shortly after 9/11, producers planned a special where Edward would connect with victims' families. The plan sparked a national outcry over exploitation of the tragedy, and the special was canceled. [30]

**Role of Skeptics**: The cancellation was primarily a **business decision** due to falling ratings, but skeptics had been actively publishing criticisms for years, which may have contributed to declining viewership:

* **James Randi** analyzed Edward's shows and found only 3 of 23 statements correct—published in TV Guide [31]
* **Skeptical Inquirer** published "John Edward: Hustling the Bereaved" (2001) by Joe Nickell [32]
* **JREF** offered Edward the $1 million challenge multiple times—he consistently refused [33]
* **James Underdown** (IIG) attended a live show in 2002 and called him "a bad cold reader" [34]
* **Pre-show questionnaires**: Critics noted that audience members filled out forms beforehand, which could constitute "hot reading" [34]

Edward never accepted Randi's $1 million challenge, unlike other psychics who at least attempted testing. The ongoing skeptic criticism may have made it harder to get renewed and contributed to the declining audience, though the primary factor was simply poor ratings performance.

Edward moved on to "John Edward Cross Country" on WE tv (2006-2009), but eventually left traditional television altogether to focus on his online platform, Evolve Plus. [23]

## How It Works: Telephathy with the Dead

Edward describes his ability as fundamentally **telepathic communication with deceased consciousness**: [3, 4]

* **Clairvoyance**: "cloudy seeing"—subtle visual impressions, like reading words without seeing them clearly
* **Clairaudience**: "cloudy hearing"—faint auditory information
* **Clairsentience**: Feeling sensations from spirit

He works with a team of spirit beings he calls "The Boys"—deceased individuals who assist him in connecting with the recently departed. This is not channeling or possession, but direct telepathic information transfer from the deceased to the medium. [3, 4]

### Client Support

Recognizing the emotional intensity of his readings, Edward maintains **counselors on staff** who are available to speak with clients after readings. This support system helps people process the emotional impact of connecting with deceased loved ones, demonstrating his commitment to ethical practice and client wellbeing.

## The HBO Experiments: 83% Accuracy Under Double-Blind Conditions

In 1999-2000, Dr. Gary Schwartz at the University of Arizona's Human Energy Systems Laboratory conducted what remains the most rigorous test of mediumship ever performed. The experiments were filmed for an HBO documentary.

**Experimental Design:** [5, 6, 7]

* Five renowned mediums participated: John Edward, Suzanne Northrop, George Anderson, Anne Gehman, and Laurie Campbell [5, 6]
* Two sitters (women who had lost multiple loved ones in the past 10 years) were selected [5, 6]
* Mediums and sitters were separated by a partition
* Mediums knew only that sitters had experienced multiple losses—no other information
* Sitters answered only "yes" or "no" questions to minimize cueing
* The research was double-blind: sitters were blind to which readings were theirs until after scoring

**Results:** [5, 6, 7]

* Average accuracy for all mediums: **83%**
* Control group (non-mediums guessing): 36%
* Individual accuracy ranged from 77% to 95%
* One medium asked only 5 questions but reported 120+ pieces of information rated over 80% accurate [5, 6, 7]

---

## The Miraval and Canyon Ranch Studies: Triple-Blind

Following the HBO experiments, Dr. Schwartz conducted even more rigorous testing: [8]

**Miraval Silent-Sitter Experiment:**

* Mediums and sitters could not see or hear each other
* First 10 minutes: sitters completely silent (no feedback at all)
* Second half: sitters could answer yes/no questions
* Results: 77% accuracy during silent portion, 83% during yes/no portion [8]

**Canyon Ranch Totally Silent Sitter:**

* Three mediums performed readings with absolutely no response from subjects
* Subject communicated via an experimenter who relayed yes/no answers
* Results: Still well above chance, though lower than previous experiments [8]

## Windbridge Institute Research (2008-Present)

John Edward has also participated in ongoing research at the Windbridge Institute (now Windbridge Research Center) in Tucson, Arizona, under the direction of Dr. Julie Beischel.

**Research Highlights:**

* **Triple-blind protocols**: Medium blind to sitter identity, experimenter blind to both, absent sitters score readings later
* **Certification program**: Edward was among the "Windbridge Certified Research Mediums" (WCRMs)
* **Ongoing studies**: The "Proof-Focused Study" continues to examine long-term accuracy
* **Phenomenology research**: Studying the subjective experiences of mediums during readings [9, 10]

Dr. Beischel's research has been published in peer-reviewed journals including Explore: The Journal of Science and Healing and the Journal of Nervous and Mental Disease.

## Other Double-Blind Studies: The Growing Body of Evidence

The John Edward and Schwartz studies are not isolated. A growing body of peer-reviewed research confirms these findings across multiple laboratories and mediums:

**Meta-Analysis (Sarraf et al., 2020)**

A comprehensive meta-analysis of 18 experiments conducted between 2001-2019, using varying levels of blinding, found:

* **Average accuracy 6-14% above chance** - statistically significant
* **Better performance** by mediums who passed preliminary certification screening
* Published in peer-reviewed literature [11]

**Tressoldi et al. (2020) - Triple-Blind Study**

Nine professional mediums produced 38 readings under rigorous triple-blind conditions:

* Mediums received only the deceased person's first name
* Sitters never interacted with mediums
* Results: **65.8% correct identification** (vs. 50% chance expected)
* Intended readings had **29.5% more correct information** than control readings
* Published in Elsevier journal [12]

**Beischel (2007) - Beyond Double-Blind**

Eight research mediums participated in a study published in Explore:

* Sitters correctly identified their intended reading **81% of the time** (p=0.01)
* Average summary rating: 3.56 for intended vs. 1.94 for control
* Effect size: 0.5 (considered moderate to large)
* Six of eight mediums produced positive results in the predicted direction [13]

## Important Distinction: Scientifically Tested vs. TV Psychics

It's worth noting that not all television psychics have submitted to scientific testing:

* **Theresa Caputo** (Long Island Medium) - Has never participated in controlled studies. In 2012, she received a **Pigasus Award** from the James Randi Educational Foundation for "performance." Investigations reveal she uses pre-show research and social media to gather information about audience members. [14]
* **Chip Coffey** - Also received a **Pigasus Award** (2009) for "the psychic who tricked the most people with the least effort." Skeptics planted fictitious dead family members with his staff, and he claimed to communicate with them successfully. [15]

The difference: Mediums like John Edward have voluntarily participated in decades of controlled testing, while others like Caputo and Coffey have refused scientific scrutiny.

## The "Chasing Evil" Book: 25 Years of FBI Collaboration

In September 2025, Edward published "Chasing Evil: Shocking Crimes, Supernatural Forces, and an FBI Agent's Search for Hope and Justice" (St. Martin's Press), co-authored with former FBI Special Agent Robert Hilland.

The book documents their 25-year partnership (1998-2023), during which Edward assisted on numerous cold cases: [16, 17]

* The "John Smith" murders (the case that started it all)
* The murder of Yale student Annie Le
* The Michael Vick dogfighting investigation
* Cases in Afghanistan and Kazakhstan

Hilland, initially a skeptic, describes how his "worldview was shattered" by the results. In one notable case, Edward directed investigators to a body hidden in wall pipes—a body that had been missed in previous searches. [16, 17]

## Critics Who Called Edward a Fraud

Several notable figures have publicly claimed John Edward uses deception: [18, 19]

### James Randi

Randi analyzed Edward's television performances and found only 3 of 23 statements correct. He called Edward a "cold reader" and published his analysis showing the majority of Edward's statements were wrong. [18, 19]

### James Underdown (Independent Investigative Group)

Underdown attended a live "Crossing Over" show in November 2002 and published his observations: [19]

> "John Edward was a bad cold reader. He, too, struggled to get hits, and in one attempt shot off nearly 40 guesses before finding any significant targets."

He also alleged that Edward's television accuracy was "inflated by the editing process" and that "Edward's editor fine-tuned many of the dead-ends out of a reading riddled with misses." [19]

### John Oliver (Last Week Tonight, 2019)

Oliver featured Edward in a segment criticizing the psychic industry, showing clips of failed cold reading attempts. He called the psychic industry "predatory" and said it "emboldens a vast underworld of unscrupulous vultures, more than happy to make money by offering an open line to the afterlife." [19]

### No Lawsuits from Edward

Unlike Uri Geller (who sued Randi multiple times for defamation), John Edward has never filed a defamation lawsuit against any of his critics. He has only responded verbally, denying that he uses cold or hot reading techniques. [19]

Edward's response to criticism has been to point to his willingness to participate in controlled laboratory testing—unlike many psychics who refuse scientific scrutiny.

### Edward's Response: Letting the Work Speak for Itself

Rather than engage in public battles with critics, John Edward has consistently taken the approach of letting his work demonstrate its validity:

**"I can't disrespect the work"**

In a 2018 interview with the Sydney Morning Herald, Edward explained his philosophy: he refuses to attack skeptics personally and instead focuses on the work itself. The title of the interview captured his sentiment—"Lunch with John Edward: I can't disrespect the work." [20]

**"Just watch me"**

When asked about his response to critics, Edward has said: "He says the critics don't bother him. 'I'm not more special than anyone else, except I have worked at what I possess for three decades. I believe that everyone has ESP-like powers.'" [21]

This approach—rather than attacking skeptics—invites them to observe his work and draw their own conclusions.

**"I've never felt the need to convince or convert people"**

In a 2015 interview with Penn Live, Edward stated: "I've never felt the need to convince or convert people. I don't think that's appropriate." He explained that he respects both believers and skeptics, and only asks that skeptics respect that others have different beliefs. [22]

This philosophy reflects Edward's core approach: rather than arguing with critics, he lets his decades of scientific participation and client work speak for itself. His participation in controlled studies (HBO experiments, Windbridge research) and his 25-year FBI collaboration are his response to skepticism—not attacks on his critics.

## Skeptics Countered

Skeptics have criticized Edward's results. Here's why their critiques don't fully explain the results:

### Against Randi's "Cold Reading" Critique

Randi analyzed Edward's television performances and found only 3 of 23 statements correct. However: [18, 19]

1. **Edited broadcasts**: Television shows edit out failures; Randi was analyzing edited content, not live performances
2. **Pre-show interviews**: Edward sometimes conducts pre-show interviews that may leak information
3. **Laboratory studies bypass these issues**: The HBO experiments, Miraval studies, and Windbridge research were specifically designed to eliminate cold reading through rigorous blinding [5, 6, 7, 8, 9]

### Why Telepathy Remains the Best Explanation

When all conventional explanations are eliminated:

* No visual or auditory contact between medium and sitter
* No information provided about the deceased
* Sitters score readings without knowing which readings are theirs
* Results significantly exceed chance

The most parsimonious explanation is that consciousness can transfer information independent of the physical senses—precisely what mediumship claims.
