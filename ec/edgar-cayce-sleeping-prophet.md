# Edgar Cayce: The Sleeping Prophet

Edgar Cayce (1877-1945) has been called the "sleeping prophet," the "father of holistic medicine," and "the most documented psychic of the 20th century." For more than 40 years of his adult life, Cayce gave psychic "readings" to thousands of seekers while in an unconscious state, diagnosing illnesses and revealing information about past lives, healing, and prophecies yet to come. [1][2]

---

## Background and Early Life

Edgar Cayce was born on a farm in Hopkinsville, Kentucky, in 1877. His psychic abilities appeared early in childhood:

* He could see and talk to his late grandfather's spirit
* He often played with "imaginary friends" whom he said were spirits on the other side
* He displayed an uncanny ability to **memorize the pages of a book simply by sleeping on it** [1][3]

These gifts labeled the young Cayce as "different" from his peers, but all Cayce really wanted was to help others. [1]

### The Birth of His Ability

According to his biography, Cayce's documented ability began after he was struck on the base of the spine by a ball in a school game. After the accident, he began to act strangely and was put to bed. He went to sleep and diagnosed the cure, which his family prepared and which cured him as he slept. However, this ability was not demonstrated again for several years. [4]

At age 21, Cayce lost his voice due to partial paralysis of his vocal cords. During his first documented psychic reading, with the help of Al Layne, he diagnosed his own difficulty while asleep and prescribed a treatment that restored his voice. [3]

---

## How He Performed Readings

Cayce's method was unique among famous psychics:

### The Trance State

Cayce would put himself into a sleep-like state by:
- Lying down on a couch
- Closing his eyes
- Folding his hands over his stomach

In this state of relaxation and meditation, he was able to place his mind in contact with all time and space—**the universal consciousness**, also known as the super-conscious mind. [1][5]

### What He Diagnosed

From this state, Cayce could respond to questions on subjects including:
- Healing and diseases
- Reincarnation
- Dreams and dream interpretation
- The afterlife
- Past lives
- Nutrition
- Atlantis and ancient mysteries
- Future events
- Spiritual growth [1][2]

### The Numbers

Over his lifetime, Cayce gave **14,306 readings** covering **10,000 different topics**. [1][2]

## Acceptance and Credibility

Unlike many psychics, Edgar Cayce gained remarkable acceptance from both the public and some medical professionals:

### Medical Recognition

* Cayce worked with doctors including Dr. Wesley Ketchum, who verified many of his diagnoses
* The majority of his readings dealt with **holistic health and the treatment of illness**
* Many people reportedly received physical relief from illnesses through information given in readings—even some given over 100 years ago [1][2]

### Publications and Books

* **1942**: Thomas Sugrue's biography ["There Is a River (1942)"](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0399172664/?tag=psychicblurbs-20) was published—the only biography written during Cayce's lifetime, which brought national attention to Cayce [4]
* **1950**: Gina Cerminara wrote ["Many Mansions (1950)"](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0440136768/?tag=psychicblurbs-20) exploring Cayce's work
* **1963**: Ruth Montgomery popularized Cayce's predictions about catastrophic events
* **1967**: Jess Stearn authored ["The Sleeping Prophet (1967)"](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0385070183/?tag=psychicblurbs-20), a bestselling biography [4]

### Influence on New Age Movement

Cayce is regarded as a **founder of the New Age movement** and a principal source of many of the movement's characteristic beliefs. [4]

## The Association for Research and Enlightenment (A.R.E.)

In 1931, Cayce founded the **Association for Research and Enlightenment (A.R.E.)**, a non-profit organization still active today. [4][6]

### What the A.R.E. Does

The organization continues to preserve and promote Cayce's work:

* Maintains the entire set of 14,306 readings in searchable databases
* Runs a health center and spa in Virginia Beach
* Publishes materials through A.R.E. Press and 4th Dimension Press
* Operates Atlantic University (nonprofit online university with masters in transpersonal studies)
* Runs study groups worldwide
* Hosts lectures and tours [6]

### Membership

Prior to Cayce's death in 1945, people seeking a reading were asked to join the A.R.E. This helped insulate Cayce from charges of fortune-telling (illegal in some U.S. states), as he was not directly charging a fee but receiving a salary from the member-supported organization. [6]

---

## His Philosophy

Cayce claimed no special abilities and never considered himself a prophet:

* He said he was a devout Christian and was **not a spiritualist or communicating with spirits** [4]
* The readings never offered a set of beliefs to be embraced
* Instead, they focused on each person testing principles in their own life
* His work stressed the **oneness of all life**, tolerance for all people, and compassion for all faiths [5]

---

## Legacy

Edgar Cayce died on January 3, 1945. His work continues to influence:

* Holistic medicine practitioners
* The New Age movement
* Dream interpretation enthusiasts
* Those interested in reincarnation and past lives
* Spiritual seekers worldwide

The A.R.E. remains active today, with headquarters in Virginia Beach, and tens of thousands of people access Cayce's material each day. [1][2][6]
