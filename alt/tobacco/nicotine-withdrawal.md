# Nicotine: Withdrawal and Cessation

Quitting nicotine produces predictable withdrawal symptoms, but effective treatments exist to help manage them.

---

## Withdrawal Symptoms

### Physical Symptoms

- Headache
- Fatigue
- Increased appetite
- Weight gain
- Gastrointestinal discomfort
- Constipation
- Dizziness [1][2]

### Psychological Symptoms

- Irritability
- Anxiety
- Depression
- Difficulty concentrating
- Restlessness [1][2]

### Behavioral

- Intense cravings
- Insomnia or sleep disturbances
- Mouth ulcers [1][2]

---

## Withdrawal Timeline

| Time Since Last Use | Symptoms |
|---------------------|----------|
| 30 minutes - 4 hours | Cravings begin, irritability starts |
| 4-24 hours | Peak cravings, headache, fatigue |
| 2-3 days | Peak physical symptoms, difficulty concentrating |
| 3-7 days | Most acute symptoms subside |
| 1-2 weeks | Lingering cravings, mood issues |
| 2-4 weeks | Most symptoms resolve, though cravings may persist |

[1][2]

## Cessation Methods

### Cold Turkey

Quitting abruptly without assistance.

- Success rates: approximately 5-10%
- Most common method but least effective [3][4]

### Nicotine Replacement Therapy (NRT)

| Product | Description | Strengths |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| Patches | Transdermal, 16-24 hour release | 21mg, 14mg, 7mg |
| Gum | Chewing, buccal absorption | 2mg, 4mg |
| Lozenges | Dissolving, oral absorption | 2mg, 4mg |
| Inhalers | Vapor, oral inhalation | 10mg cartridges |
| Nasal spray | Fast absorption | 10mg/ml |

**Effectiveness**: Combining products (patches + gum/lozenges) is more effective than single products. [3][5][6]

### Prescription Medications

#### Varenicline (Chantix/Chantix)

- Partial nicotinic receptor agonist
- Reduces cravings and blocks nicotine effects
- Must be prescribed by a healthcare provider [7][8]

#### Bupropion (Zyban)

- Antidepressant that reduces nicotine cravings
- Also requires prescription
- May be combined with NRT [7][8]

### Behavioral Therapy

- Counseling and support groups significantly improve quit rates
- Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT) helps identify triggers
- Telephone quitlines (1-800-QUIT-NOW) provide free support [3][9]

### E-Cigarettes

- Some adults use e-cigarettes as a cessation tool
- Controversial—NOT FDA-approved for cessation
- Some evidence suggests effectiveness, but concerns about youth uptake remain [3][10]
