# JUUL: A Case Study in Controversy

JUUL Labs became the dominant e-cigarette brand in the United States, capturing approximately 75% of the market by 2019. However, its rise was accompanied by significant controversy and legal scrutiny.

---

## Product Details

- **Device**: Sleek, rectangular vaping device with magnetic pod attachment
- **Pods (JUULpods)**: Pre-filled, closed-system nicotine cartridges
- **Nicotine concentration**: 5% (59 mg/ml)—significantly higher than the EU/UK cap of 20 mg/ml
- **Nicotine formula**: Nicotine salts (chemical formulation that reduces harshness, enabling higher concentrations)
- **Nicotine content**: One JUUL pod contains approximately the same nicotine as 20 cigarettes

[1][2][3]

## Company Status

### Headquarters

- **Current HQ**: Washington, D.C. (1000 F St NW, 8th Floor)
- **Former HQ**: San Francisco, California (123 Mission Street)
- The company purchased the 29-story, 360,000 square foot building in 2019 for $397 million
- Abandoned the office less than a year later, listed it for sale expecting $450 million
- No successful purchase offers materialized
- In March 2026, investors (Madison Capital and PGIM) bought the debt for $90-100 million (less than 25% of pre-pandemic value)
- The company relocated to Washington, D.C., likely for lobbying purposes given regulatory scrutiny [45][46][47]

### Current Business Status

**Yes, JUUL is still in business in the USA.**

In **July 2025**, the FDA granted JUUL marketing authorization for their products:

- **FDA Decision**: July 17, 2025 - Marketing Granted Orders (MGOs) for the JUUL System [49][50]
- **Products Authorized**: JUUL device + Virginia Tobacco and Menthol flavored JUULpods (3% and 5% nicotine) [49]
- **Data**: JUUL submitted 110+ scientific studies, including a 2-year longitudinal cohort study showing 38-52% of adult smokers switched completely to JUUL [49][50]
- **Note**: FDA originally ordered a ban in 2022 but reversed course days later. The authorization is not "FDA approval" - it means the products meet the "appropriate for protection of public health" standard [49][50][51]

## FDA Warning Letter (September 9, 2019)

The FDA issued a warning letter citing violations of Section 911 of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic (FD&C) Act:

### Violations

- Marketing unauthorized modified risk tobacco products
- Claiming products were "99% safer than cigarettes"
- Claiming products were "much safer than cigarettes"
- Describing products as "totally safe"
- Marketing as "a safer alternative than smoking cigarettes"

[6][7]

### FDA Questions

- Why use nicotine salts? (reduces harshness, enables ~3x higher concentration)
- Why 5% nicotine concentration?
- School presentations telling students JUUL was "totally safe"

[1][6]

## "Nicotine is a Drug" Argument

Public health organizations argued JUUL should be regulated as a drug/device:

- Marketing implied the product helped smokers "switch" or quit—therapeutic claims requiring FDA drug approval
- Under the FDA's "Tobacco Intended Use Rule," products marketed for smoking cessation require approval as drugs/devices
- Selling without requisite drug approval = illegal marketing under the FD&C Act [13][14]
