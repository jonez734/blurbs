# Rachel Maddow

Rachel Anne Maddow (born April 1, 1973) is an American television host, author, and liberal political commentator. She hosts *The Rachel Maddow Show* on MS NOW (formerly MSNBC) and serves as the network's special event co-anchor [maddow-1]. She is known for long, research-heavy opening segments, which she calls the "A-block," and for a style that combines deep reporting with historical analogy and dry humor [maddow-1][maddow-2].

## Early life and education

Maddow was born in Castro Valley, California. Her father, Robert B. Maddow, was a former United States Air Force captain who later worked as a lawyer for the East Bay Municipal Utility District. Her mother, Elaine (née Gosse), was a school program administrator [maddow-1].

She attended Castro Valley High School and was a competitive athlete in volleyball, basketball, and swimming [maddow-1]. She enrolled at Stanford University in 1990 and earned a bachelor's degree in public policy in 1994 [maddow-1][maddow-2]. She was a Rhodes Scholar and began postgraduate study at Lincoln College, Oxford, in 1995. She turned down a Marshall Scholarship to take the Rhodes. She earned a DPhil in politics from Oxford in 2001. Her thesis, supervised by Lucia Zedner, was titled "HIV/AIDS and Health Care Reform in British and American Prisons" [maddow-1].

## Family and personal life

Maddow has one older brother, David [maddow-1]. Her partner is artist Susan Mikula; the two met in 1999, when Maddow was working on her dissertation. The two split their time between Manhattan and western Massachusetts [maddow-1][maddow-2].

She came out publicly as a lesbian in a Stanford student newspaper interview while still a freshman, before she had told her parents [maddow-1][maddow-3]. She has described herself as a practicing Catholic and has said her family is "very, very Catholic" [maddow-1]. She has spoken openly about cyclical depression, which she has lived with since puberty [maddow-3].

## Radio and early career

Maddow's first broadcasting job was in 1999 at WRNX (100.9 FM) in Holyoke, Massachusetts, where she won a station contest to become the second host of a morning show [maddow-1][maddow-2]. She later hosted a program on WRSI in Northampton, Massachusetts [maddow-1]. In 2004 she joined the new Air America Radio network. There she co-hosted *Unfiltered*, and her own two-hour program, *The Rachel Maddow Show*, ran until Air America ceased operations in January 2010 [maddow-1][maddow-3].

## Television

In 2005 Maddow became a regular panelist on MSNBC's *Tucker*, hosted by Tucker Carlson [maddow-1]. In 2008 she substituted for Keith Olbermann on *Countdown*, and MSNBC then gave her the 9:00 p.m. Eastern slot that September [maddow-1]. After its debut the show topped *Countdown* as MSNBC's highest-rated show on several occasions [maddow-1].

By 2017 *The Rachel Maddow Show* was described as the number-one prime-time news program on cable television [maddow-2]. In 2022 the show moved to a weekly broadcast. In 2025 MSNBC, by then renamed MS NOW, brought her back to five nights a week for the first 100 days of Donald Trump's second presidency, and she later returned to a Monday-only schedule [maddow-1].

## Writing

- *Drift: The Unmooring of American Military Power* (2012). On the role of the military and intelligence agencies in postwar American politics. It reached the top of the *New York Times* hardcover nonfiction list [maddow-1][maddow-3].
- *Blowout: Corrupted Democracy, Rogue State Russia, and the Richest, Most Destructive Industry on Earth* (2019) [maddow-1].
- *Bag Man: The Wild Crimes, Audacious Cover-up, and Spectacular Downfall of a Brazen Crook in the White House* (2020), written with Michael Yarvitz [maddow-1].
- *Prequel: An American Fight Against Fascism* (2023), based on her *Ultra* podcast [maddow-1].

In 2013 *The Washington Post* announced a six-month run of monthly opinion columns by Maddow. In 2018 the *New York Times* published her first crossword puzzle [maddow-1].

## Podcasts

- ***Bag Man*** (2018). An investigation into the forced resignation of Vice President Spiro Agnew, produced with MSNBC [maddow-1].
- ***Ultra*** (2022). A series on American right-wing extremism during the 1940s and World War II, including the 1944 sedition trial. Steven Spielberg's company later optioned film rights to the series [maddow-1].
- ***Burn Order*** (December 2025). A history of the internment of Japanese Americans during World War II and the government cover-up that followed. Maddow presented it as her first project for MS NOW [maddow-1][maddow-4].

## Awards and honors

- **Grammy Award**, Best Spoken Word Album, for the audiobook of *Blowout* (2021). Earlier, she was nominated for *Drift* (2013) [maddow-1].
- **Emmy Awards** (2011, 2017) for news discussion and analysis and for live interviews on her show [maddow-1].
- **Alfred I. duPont-Columbia University Award** (2020) for *Bag Man* [maddow-1].
- **Hillman Prize for Broadcast Journalism** (2023) for *Ultra*. This was the first time the prize went to a podcast [maddow-1].
- **Peabody Award nomination** (2018) for *Bag Man* [maddow-1].
- **Walter Cronkite Faith & Freedom Award** (2010), presented by the Interfaith Alliance [maddow-1].
- **Gracie Allen Award**, Outstanding Host (2012) [maddow-1].
- Honorary Doctor of Laws from Smith College, where she gave the 2010 commencement address [maddow-1].

## Philosophy and public views

Maddow describes herself as a liberal. In 2017 she said, "Oh, I'm a liberal for sure. I'm just not a candidate person," and said she was "not a huge fan of the Democratic Party" [maddow-2]. Wikipedia describes her self-description as a "national security liberal" [maddow-1].

She has stated her show's mission as "Increase the amount of useful information in the world" [maddow-2]. Her approach to the Trump administration was summed up in a rule she gave in 2017: "Don't pay attention to what they say, focus on what they do" [maddow-2].

On the press, she has said: "I am a cheerleader for the American media and I feel like the free press is going to be what saves us from the political crisis that we are in" [maddow-2]. In 2012 she argued that secrecy in national security lets elected officials avoid accountability: "Politically, secrecy is a great excuse" [maddow-3].

She has defended humor as a teaching tool. In 2012 she said, "I think that humor has underappreciated explanatory value" [maddow-3]. In 2025 she said, "Doing the right thing doesn't always pay off in the short run, but your country will ultimately get this right" [maddow-4].
