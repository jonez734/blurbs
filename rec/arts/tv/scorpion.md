# Scorpion — "We're Gonna Need a Bigger Vote"

**Usenet Groups:** rec.arts.tv (primary), alt.tv.scorpion (secondary)

---

## Overview

Scorpion was an American action-drama television series that aired on CBS from September 22, 2014 to April 16, 2018, created by Nick Santora and loosely based on the life of Irish computer prodigy Walter O'Brien. The show followed a team of genius misfits working as a Homeland Security think tank solving high-tech threats, with a "normal" single mother (Katharine McPhee) acting as their translator to the outside world.

The series ran for 4 seasons and 93 episodes, falling just seven episodes short of the traditional 100-episode syndication benchmark.

## Episode Spotlight: "We're Gonna Need a Bigger Vote"

- **Season/Episode:** Season 3, Episode 7 (53rd overall)
- **Air Date:** November 7, 2016 — Election Day 2016
- **Production Number:** 307
- **Writers:** Elizabeth Davis Beall & Nick Santora
- **Director:** Sam Hill
- **Title Reference:** A play on Chief Brody's iconic line from *Jaws* ("You're gonna need a bigger boat")

### Plot

On Election Day 2016, the U.S. presidential vote-counting system begins behaving erratically, and Team Scorpion is called in to stop a sabotage operation before democracy implodes. Walter O'Brien has been barred from the case by an immigration officer scrutinizing his green-card marriage to Happy (which the team previously staged for citizenship purposes), so he and Ralph set up a makeshift base in a warehouse while the rest of the team works the crime.

The trail leads from an overheating vote tabulator to a frisbee-sized transmitter planted on the wall, to a Chinese embassy hack via a compromised aide's tablet. Meanwhile, Toby is sent on air as a panelist to create a distraction — and promptly loses his mind in a glorious, unhinged political rant that delights reviewers to this day. Cabe eventually grabs the hacker's laptop at the embassy gate and uses a recorded confession tape as leverage to walk out unmolested.

### Subplots

- **The Paigening:** Paige agrees to meet boyfriend Tim's parents for dinner; Ralph tells Walter he wouldn't mind Tim as a stepfather, but Walter's jealousy is unmistakable.
- **Citizenship:** The President personally signs Walter's citizenship paperwork. Happy is unmoved.
- **Toby & Happy:** A false-positive pregnancy (from blood contamination while working solar panels) leads to emotional devastation — and Toby proposes anyway. She says yes.

### Notable Moments

- **Ralph:** "You're what my teacher calls the crackpot vote."
- **Happy:** "My top issue on election day? Surveillance. The bad guys watch our every move, but who watches the watchers?"
- **Toby's on-air meltdown** — routinely cited in fan retrospectives as one of the show's best comedic beats.
- **The Plot Hole:** Reviewers (TV Fanatic in particular) complained that the Secret Service somehow didn't have a photo ID for the pre-cleared caterer planting the device, despite Walter needing to get on Air Force One.

## General Show Info

### Premise

Inspired by the real Walter O'Brien's company Concierge Up, the show was pitched as a 10-season plan to "compete with CSI" and "inspire a whole generation to see that 'smart is cool.'" CBS leadership openly framed it as "kind of an extension of *The Big Bang Theory* but in the drama world," per then-CBS Primetime SVP Kelly Kahl.

### The Team

- **Walter O'Brien** (Elyes Gabel) — Eccentric genius team leader, emotionally constipated
- **Toby Curtis** (Eddie Kaye Thomas) — Behaviorist who can read anyone; Walter's best friend and eventual husband of Happy
- **Happy Quinn** (Jadyn Wong) — Mechanical prodigy
- **Sylvester Dodd** (Ari Stidham) — Statistics/math whiz with a heart of gold
- **Cabe Gallo** (Robert Patrick) — Former FBI agent, team handler, and Walter's long-time protector
- **Paige Dineen** (Katharine McPhee) — Single mom whose late husband was a Team Scorpion protégé; she translates the world for the geniuses
- **Ralph Dineen** (Riley B. Smith) — Paige's gifted son, the emotional heart of the show

## Amusing Anecdotes

- **The McPhee–Gabel Romance:** Co-stars Elyes Gabel and Katharine McPhee began dating on set and were together for roughly two years before splitting in early 2016. It remains a popular fandom trivia point.
- **Musical Genius:** Gabel and McPhee performed an impromptu karaoke rendition of Elton John's "Don't Go Breaking My Heart" in the S2 episode "Sun of a Gun," and Gabel later performed an original musical number in the S4 episode "Extinction." Way back in 2005, Gabel sang The Beatles' "I Saw Her Standing There" on BBC's *Children in Need* with co-performer Luke Bailey.
- **Walter O'Brien's Vision:** The real O'Brien reportedly pitched CBS a 10-season arc intended to compete directly with *CSI*.
- **Sylvester for Office:** This episode introduced the running gag where Sylvester is inspired to run for local office to save his beloved comic book shop from demolition.
- **The "Marriage" Gag:** Walter and Happy's sham marriage for immigration purposes became one of the show's longest-running plot devices, directly setting up Walter's benching in this very episode.
- **Behind Air Force One:** The fact that Walter had to sneak onto Air Force One to resolve the case was widely mocked by reviewers — *TV Fanatic* asked whether *anyone else* was bothered that Walter could simply walk onto the presidential plane.
- **Charity Work:** The cast was active in CBS Cares campaigns for Autism awareness, anti-bullying (AFN), Multiple Sclerosis, and ALS (Gabel walked the ALS Golden West Chapter event in 2014 alongside Renée Zellweger and Reese Witherspoon).

## Reasons for Cancellation

CBS axed *Scorpion* on May 12, 2018, the same day *Superior Donuts* got the chop. Contributing factors:

1. **The 32% Drop.** Season 4 averaged a 0.81 in the 18–49 demo, down 32% from Season 3, and the finale drew just 5.1 million viewers and an all-time-low 0.7 demo.
2. **No Syndication Deal.** Despite four seasons, the show never secured the off-network syndication package CBS expected. Coming in seven episodes under the magic 100 mark hurt.
3. **The 10 pm Death Slot.** After CBS rebuilt Monday as a two-hour comedy block in fall 2016, *Scorpion* was bounced from its cushy post-*Big Bang Theory* 9 pm slot to the brutal 10 pm hour, where it absorbed two consecutive ~32% ratings losses.
4. **Lead-In Collapse.** *2 Broke Girls* and eventually *Me, Myself & I* / *9JKL* failed to give it a runway.
5. **Renewal Freeze.** CBS renewed 11 of its current series in April 2018 — *Scorpion* was conspicuously absent, downgrading it from "safe bet" to "could go either way" within weeks.

*Scorpion* was the 20th broadcast series cancelled in the 2017–18 cycle.

### U.S. Off-Network Syndication

Unlike most four-season CBS procedurals, *Scorpion* never secured a traditional domestic broadcast syndication deal — the 93-episode shortfall below the 100-episode benchmark was widely cited as the culprit. The show's afterlife has been primarily through streaming rather than weekday broadcast reruns.

## Where Are They Now?

**Elyes Gabel** (Walter O'Brien)
- Signed with ICM Partners in November 2017 ahead of the show's final season
- Post-cancellation credits limited to voice work (*Justice League vs. the Fatal Five*) and a recurring role on Apple TV+'s *Suspicion* (2022)
- Continues his charitable work with the ALS Association in honor of his late grandmother Noreen Gurney

**Katharine McPhee** (Paige Dineen)
- Broadway debut as Jenna in *Waitress* (2018), shortly after the show's cancellation
- Competed on *The Masked Singer* (Season 1, 2019) — placed third in a duo with mentor David Foster
- Married David Foster in June 2019
- Released standards album *I Fall in Love Too Easily* (2017) and 2022 EP *Christmas Songs*

**Robert Patrick** (Cabe Gallo)
- The busiest post-*Scorpion* career of the cast
- Film credits include *Sgt. Will Gardner*, *The Poison Rose*, *The Rising Hawk*, *The Laundromat*, *Tone-Deaf*, and *Honest Thief* opposite Liam Neeson

**Eddie Kaye Thomas** (Toby Curtis)
- Appeared on *This Is Us*, *Outmatched*, and *Law & Order: SVU*

**Jadyn Wong** (Happy Quinn)
- Continued acting in smaller roles after the series ended

**Ari Stidham** (Sylvester Dodd)
- Kept a lower profile post-cancellation

**Riley B. Smith** (Ralph Dineen)
- Was a child actor during the run; his post-*Scorpion* credits have been limited

## Fan Legacy

- Despite its rocky ratings trajectory, *Scorpion* maintained a devoted fanbase that rallied on social media under **#TeamScorpion** after cancellation. McPhee publicly thanked "the wonderful @ScorpionCBS fans for being so passionate about what we created. we'll always be a family."
- The show is often cited alongside *The Big Bang Theory* and *Young Sheldon* as part of CBS's brief "smart is cool" programming push.
- Its win at the Monte-Carlo Television Festival just weeks after cancellation gave fans a small vindication — and gave CBS an extra postmortem trophy to point to.

## Trivia

- The title "We're Gonna Need a Bigger Vote" is one of several *Jaws*-referencing episode titles across CBS's procedural lineup of the era.
- The episode's Chinese-embassy hack plot was considered unusually topical for the series, airing on the literal Election Day 2016.
- Walter's citizenship subplot resolved in this episode was the culmination of a multi-season green-card-marriage arc with Happy.
- This is the episode where Sylvester decides to run for local office to save his comic book shop — a recurring B-plot for the rest of the series.
