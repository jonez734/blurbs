# Major Sidney "Sid" Freedman

**Dr. Sidney Freedman** is the 4077th's most welcome occasional visitor. Played by character actor **Allan Arbus** (1918–2013) across twelve episodes of the series, Freedman is a U.S. Army psychiatrist who periodically descends on the 4077th to talk the camp's surgeons—and occasionally Colonel Potter—through the psychological wreckage the Korean War leaves in its wake. A civilian-trained psychoanalyst drafted into uniform, Freedman is calm where everyone else is frantic, patient where everyone else is defensive, and disarmingly honest in a way that cuts through the camp's gallows humor. He is the only character to recur on the show who is never actually stationed at the 4077th; he arrives, helps, and departs [1].

Freedman is the 4077th's resident conscience—the character the show reaches for when a story turns genuinely dark, whether it's Hawkeye's breakdown, a patient who can't stop reliving what he saw, or BJ's crisis of confidence. His sessions with patients and colleagues are played almost entirely straight; Freedman himself is dry, wry, and unflappable, with a gift for asking the one question nobody else thought to ask. His rapport with the 4077th's staff—especially Hawkeye, and eventually BJ—is one of the show's most quietly moving recurring threads, and his periodic appearances became a fan-favorite signal that an episode was going to deal with something real [1][2].

## Appearances

Freedman appears in twelve *M\*A\*S\*H* episodes across most of the series run. Using the broadcast-order numbering from MASH4077TV's episode guide (which differs in some places from Wikipedia's numbering):

- "Deal Me Out" (S2E13, December 8, 1973)
- "Change of Command" (S2E20, February 1, 1974)
- "Quo Vadis, Captain Chandler?" (S4E09, November 7, 1975)
- "Hawkeye" (S4E18, January 27, 1976)
- "Out of Sight, Out of Mind" (S5E03, September 27, 1976)
- "Lend a Hand" (S6E15, January 10, 1978)
- "In Love and War" (S6E20, February 24, 1978)
- "Major Topper" (S7E05, October 16, 1978)
- "C\*A\*V\*E" (S7E11, November 27, 1978)
- "Your Retention, Please" (S8E11, November 19, 1979)
- "Nurse Doctor" (S9E04, October 22, 1980)
- "Strange Behavior" (S10E04, October 27, 1981)

In addition, Arbus appeared as Freedman in the *AfterMASH* episode "Dreams," picking up the role a few years after the Korean War ends [1][2].

## The Flagg–Freedman Pairing

The most distinctive feature of Freedman's run on the show is how often he appears in the same story as **Colonel Flagg** (Edward Winter). In two episodes—"Deal Me Out" (S2E13) and "Quo Vadis, Captain Chandler?" (S4E09)—the two characters share the screen, with Flagg playing the suspicious intelligence officer and Freedman playing the sympathetic psychiatrist investigating the same patient. The pairing functions as a recurring comic and thematic device: the show's two outsiders, one pursuing paranoia and the other pursuing understanding, circling the same case from opposite directions [1][2].

The "Deal Me Out" appearance is particularly significant. Winter's character in that episode is never named Flagg—he's called **Captain Halloran**—but most fans of the show, and Wikipedia, treat Halloran as Flagg's actual first appearance on the show, with the two appearances linked through the Freedman connection. In "Quo Vadis, Captain Chandler?" Flagg greets Freedman like an old acquaintance, and many viewers read the moment as a quiet in-joke acknowledging the earlier pairing [1].

## Allan Arbus

Arbus (February 15, 1918 – April 19, 2013) was an unlikely late-blooming television presence. A former U.S. Army first lieutenant who served in the Pacific during World War II, Arbus was for many years better known as the husband of photographer Diane Arbus (they separated in 1959 and divorced in 1969) than for his own work. He didn't begin acting professionally until his mid-forties. The role of Sidney Freedman—cast in 1973 when Arbus was 55—became the defining part of his career, and he played the character on and off for nearly a decade [3].

Beyond *M\*A\*S\*H*, Arbus racked up guest spots on *Grey's Anatomy* (as a cardiac patient in a 2005 episode that earned him an Emmy nomination at age 87), *Law & Order*, *Mad About You*, and *Curb Your Enthusiasm*, among many others. He continued acting into his nineties and was working on the film *The King of Naples* at the time of his death in 2013, at age 95 [3].
