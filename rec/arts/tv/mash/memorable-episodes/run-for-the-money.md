# "Run for the Money"

Season 11, Episode 9 (December 20, 1982). Teleplay by Elias Davis & David Pollock; Story by Mike Farrell, Elias Davis & David Pollock; Directed by Nell Cox.

Klinger hatches his "foolproof" scheme when a champion sprinter is assigned to the 4077th: challenge rival unit the 8063rd to a race and clean up on the bets. He ropes Hawkeye, B.J., and Margaret into wagering a week's pay each—but when the new arrival turns out to be the sprinter's out-of-shape father, the camp must pin its hopes (and money) on an unlikely ringer: Father Mulcahy. The good padre agrees to run on one condition—if he wins, the engineers rebuild the orphanage's crumbling roof.

Meanwhile, Winchester takes Private Walter Palmer (Phil Brock), a wounded engineer mocked for his severe stutter, under his wing. Palmer has an above-average IQ but has been called "dumb" and "Porky" so often—by his fellow engineers and even his commander, Captain Sweeney—that he believes it himself and reads only comic books [1].

Winchester overhears the mockery and confronts Sweeney, warning that he'll write a detailed report of his "inhumanity" for his permanent record. Then he takes Palmer for what he says are x-rays, but it's really a ruse for a private conversation. He assures Palmer a speech impediment is no measure of intelligence—he can aspire to far more than comic books. To prove it, Winchester confides that he himself reads Captain Marvel, then gifts Palmer a treasured leather-bound hardcover of **Moby-Dick**.

When Palmer asks why Winchester is being so nice, he deflects and takes him back to Post Op. The episode's final scene reveals why: Winchester returns to the Swamp and listens to an audio tape from his sister Honoria—and we learn that **she also stutters**. The look of contentment on his face shows his connection to Palmer.

Notably, actor **David Ogden Stiers** (who played Winchester) stuttered throughout his childhood, adolescence, and early career. He said being an actor helped cure him: *"I didn't stutter when the lines were written for me. Without lines to read was another story... I overcame it by not giving up, by continuing to play roles."* Stiers explained that Winchester would never admit to acts of kindness—it was part of his "glacial exterior" character trait.

A warm, funny episode about wagers won and hearts even bigger, capped by a twist that leaves schemer Klinger racing to cancel his bets.
